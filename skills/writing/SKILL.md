# Writing

Help the recipient understand the intended meaning without distortion. Use this guidance in ordinary conversation as well as when creating, revising, or reviewing written material.

## Audience

- For conversation, explanations, or documents addressed to people, read [human.md](references/human.md).
- For messages, instructions, or records that guide another agent, read [agent.md](references/agent.md).

Choose by how the content will be used. Human review of an agent instruction does not change its audience. Read both branches only when the output has parts serving both uses.

## Meaning and organization

- Preserve the source's distinct information within the requested scope, including conditions, exceptions, reasons, and uncertainty. Clarify or reorganize it as needed; brevity is not a reason to discard information.
- Explain shared conditions and define terms once, using an existing authoritative definition when available. Refer back with stable names, labels, or links that the recipient can resolve. Keep a definition and its qualifications together.
- Use familiar, literal language and consistent terms. Make the subject, action, and logical relationships clear; add supported context where the recipient would otherwise have to guess. Follow the requested language.
- Use Markdown to expose relationships: headings for groups, lists for distinct items, numbering for sequence or easy reference, and tables for comparison. Keep each explanation coherent and choose only the structure the content needs.

## Check

Compare the output with the request and source: is the information complete for the requested scope, can the recipient follow the references, and could the wording imply a different meaning or action? Resolve unintended ambiguity and surface what remains unknown. Deliver the requested answer, artifact, or review without routine narration of the writing process.
